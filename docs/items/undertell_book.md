> Undertell: Its Ghosts and History

Few places in Dhayavar inspire as much dread and fascination as the caverns beneath Mt. Galmore. Known collectively as Undertell, these depths have claimed the lives of miners, soldiers, scholars, and fortune-seekers alike.

What follows is a collected account of the known and suspected truths of Undertell, compiled from fragmented records, hearsay, and personal investigation.

Chapter I: The Mines Beneath the Mountain

It is beyond dispute that Undertell was once heavily mined. Gold veins, rare gems, and other valuable stones were extracted here for decades. Even now, abandoned tunnels and collapsed shafts bear unmistakable marks of pick and flame.

Though the mines were eventually sealed, it is widely believed that much wealth remains undisturbed in the deeper reaches. Fear, not scarcity, ended the mining operations.

Those brave or foolish enough to venture below still return with traces of gold, confirming that the mountain has not yet given up all it holds.

Chapter II: Miner's Sickness

Those who labored in the caverns beneath Undertell spoke of a sickness that came not from the air or the water, but from the stones themselves. At first, it was subtle. Miners complained of a bitter taste that lingered on the tongue after long hours in the tunnels. Their breath grew sour, and their sleep was broken by strange dreams of distant fires, of voices echoing through stone, of light burning behind closed eyes.

The sickness did not strike all who entered Undertell, only those who worked near the deeper seams, where the heartstone grew thickest in the rock. Those who merely passed through the caverns did not feel its touch. Those who lingered, who carved it free with bare hands and iron tools, carried something back with them when they returned to the surface. Not a curse.

Chapter III: On Ghosts and Lingering Spirits

Many claim Undertell is haunted. Travelers speak of pale figures watching from afar, whispers echoing through stone and shapes that vanish when approached.

Some insist these are the dead miners, bound by unfinished labor. Others claim the spirits are something older, drawn to the mountain long before any pick struck rock.

There is no agreement on whether these ghosts are hostile. Accounts vary wildly, some explorers report being ignored entirely, while others describe sudden violence without warning.

Chapter IV: The Lethgar and the Elytharans

Two distinct groups of spirits are frequently mentioned in surviving accounts.

The Lethgar are described as orderly, almost disciplined, haunting areas closest to the old mining routes. Some believe they were guards or overseers in life.

The Elytharans, by contrast, appear scattered and erratic. Their presence is strongest near collapsed tunnels and forgotten chambers. Whether they are miners, prisoners, or something else entirely remains unclear.

It is possible these distinctions are imagined by fearful minds seeking patterns where none exist.

Chapter V: Of Darkness and the Shadow

Many who venture into Undertell report an oppressive presence - a weight that presses upon the mind and spirit alike. Some describe it as the Shadow itself, seeping from the stone.

Others argue this sensation is merely the result of isolation, darkness, and lingering fear passed down through generations.

There is no proof that Undertell is the source of the Shadow, only that the two are often spoken of together.

Chapter VI: Creatures of the Deep

Beyond spirits, there are reports of creatures inhabiting the lower caverns. Some resemble twisted men, others living shadows given form.

Whether these beings are monsters, spirits, or something else entirely is a matter of debate. Few witnesses survive long enough to offer clear descriptions.

Still, enough similar accounts exist to suggest that Undertell is not empty and that something watches those who descend too far.

Chapter VII: A Warning to the Curious

Undertell rewards greed as often as it punishes it. For every tale of riches recovered, there are three of those who never returned.

If knowledge is sought, tread carefully. If wealth is desired, expect to pay for it.
